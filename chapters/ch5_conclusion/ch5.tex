\chapter{Conclusion}\label{ch:conclusion}

This dissertation shows that hidden states still reveal part of the prompt in split inference, where a client sends hidden states to the server instead of the text of its prompt.
Prior attacks on per-token hidden states hold the weights of the model that produced them, or query it.
The adversary studied here has neither, and it reconstructs a prompt in part from the hidden states and a small leak of text.

The attack rests on the similarity of representations across models.
A translation hub builds on that similarity: it maps the hidden states of three open-weight language models into one shared space, and each of the three models, kept frozen, reconstructs a prompt from the hub outputs of the other two.
Mean cross-model word-F1 is \vf{0.944} on NQ, against \vf{0.120} for the mismatched-prompt control.
The hub's shared space therefore holds the prompt information of all three models.

A target model outside the hub reaches that space through a single affine map.
The map learns from two sources: a leak of \vf{90} rows and a matching term.
The matching term aligns unpaired hidden states with unpaired hub outputs and reads no text of the client.
The leak alone carries reconstruction on NQ to a word-F1 of \vf{0.35}, and the matching term lifts it to \vf{0.77}.
The hybrid fit also exceeds the supervised fit on both legal datasets.
The gain depends on the size of the leak: it is largest at \vf{45} to \vf{90} rows.
Without a leak, the matching term stays at the level of its control in the tested NQ setting.
In the tested settings, unpaired matching thus extends a small leak and does not replace it.

Three properties distinguish the attack from prior work.
First, it operates on per-token hidden states, the representation that a split inference client transmits, and it requires neither white-box nor query access to the target model.
Second, it is lightweight: one affine map is the only module trained for the target model, fitted on a leak of \vf{90} rows and on unpaired hidden states, with the hub frozen.
Third, it quantifies what unpaired matching adds to a fixed leak, a question that, to the author's knowledge, prior alignment attacks have not evaluated on per-token hidden states.
Full recovery remains challenging, with an exact match of \vf{0.21} on NQ, and the evidence covers one undefended target model observed at one block; Chapter~\ref{ch:future} takes up both.
The results therefore shift where the privacy of split inference rests: on what the representations of different models share, and not only on who holds the weights.
