\chapter{Conclusion}\label{ch:conclusion}

This dissertation shows that hidden states still reveal part of the prompt in split inference, where a client sends hidden states to the server instead of the text of its prompt.
Prior attacks on per-token hidden states hold the weights of the model that produced them, or query it.
The adversary studied here has neither, and it reconstructs a prompt in part from the hidden states and a small leak of text.

The attack rests on the similarity of representations across models.
A translation hub builds on that similarity: it maps the hidden states of three open-weight language models into one shared space, and each of the three models, kept frozen, reconstructs a prompt from the hub outputs of the other two.
Mean cross-model word-F1 is \vf{0.944} on NQ, against \vf{0.120} for the mismatched-prompt control.
The hub's shared space therefore holds the prompt information of all three models.

A target model outside the hub reaches that space through a single map.
That map is fitted on a small leak of paired text and hidden states and, beyond the leak, by distribution matching against the hub outputs of public prompts, which reads no text of the client.
With a leak of \vf{90} tokens, the supervised fit reaches a word-F1 of \vf{0.35} on NQ, and the matching term lifts it to \vf{0.77}.
The hybrid fit also exceeds the supervised fit on both legal datasets.
The gain depends on the size of the leak: it is largest at \vf{45} to \vf{90} tokens.
Without a leak, the matching term stays at the level of its control in the tested NQ setting, so distribution matching extends a small leak in these experiments and alone it fails.

The attack differs from prior work in what it reads and in what it needs: prior attacks on per-token hidden states hold the weights of the target model or query it, and the attacks that need neither read one pooled embedding per text.
It operates on per-token hidden states, the representation that a split inference client transmits, and it requires neither white-box nor query access to the target model.
A small leak and unpaired public prompts are enough to connect the target model to the hub, which stays frozen.
The measurements also report what prior alignment attacks, to the author's knowledge, have not evaluated on per-token hidden states: how much distribution matching adds to a fixed leak.
Full recovery remains challenging, with an exact match of \vf{0.21} on NQ, and the evidence covers one undefended target model observed at one block; Chapter~\ref{ch:future} takes up both.
The privacy of split inference therefore depends on what the representations of different models share, as well as on who holds the weights.
