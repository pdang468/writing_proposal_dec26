\chapter{Conclusion}\label{ch:conclusion}

This dissertation shows that hidden states still reveal part of the prompt in split inference, where a client sends hidden states to the server instead of the text of its prompt.
Prior inversion attacks hold the weights of the model that produced the hidden states, or query it.
The adversary studied here has neither, and it reconstructs a prompt in part from the hidden states and a small leak of text.

The attack rests on representations that models share.
A translation hub maps the hidden states of three open-weight language models into one shared space, and each of the three models, kept frozen, reconstructs a prompt from the hub outputs of the other two.
Mean cross-model word-F1 is \vf{0.944} on NQ, against at most \vf{0.152} for the mismatched-prompt control.
One shared space therefore holds the prompt information of all three models.

A target model outside the hub reaches that space through a single affine map.
Two sources train the map: a leak of \vf{90} rows and a matching term that aligns unpaired hidden states with unpaired hub outputs and reads no text of the client.
The leak alone carries reconstruction on NQ to a word-F1 of \vf{0.35}, and the matching term lifts it to \vf{0.77}.
The matching term raises word-F1 on both legal datasets as well, and its contribution shrinks as the leak grows.
Without a leak, the matching term stays at the level of its control in the tested NQ setting.
Unpaired matching thus extends a small leak and does not replace it.

These results establish partial reconstruction, not the recovery of complete prompts.
Exact match under the hybrid fit is \vf{0.21} on NQ and near zero on the legal datasets.
The evidence also covers one target model observed at one block, the settings of the attack were selected on prompts with known text outside the leak, and no defended system is evaluated.
Within those bounds, the privacy of split inference depends on what the representations of different models share, and not only on who holds the weights.
