\section{Size of the Leak}\label{sec:attack:budget}

Across the six leak sizes tested, the gain from matching rises with the leak, is largest at \vf{45} or \vf{90} rows, and falls as the leak grows further (Figure~\ref{fig:attack:budget}).
The comparison repeats the main comparison at six leak sizes, the first \vf{9} rows of \vf{1}, \vf{3}, \vf{5}, \vf{10}, \vf{30}, and \vf{100} prompts, or \vf{9} to \vf{900} rows.
The setting stays at $\alpha = 0.9$, $\tau = 2d$, and $B = 8{,}192$, and each fit is scored on the selection prompts.
On NQ, the gain is \vf{0.354} at \vf{27} rows, peaks at \vf{0.418} at \vf{45} rows, and falls to \vf{0.089} at \vf{900} rows.
The gain on legal10w follows the same course, with a peak of \vf{0.379} at \vf{90} rows and a fall to \vf{0.089} at \vf{900} rows.
The gain on legal100t peaks lower, at \vf{0.206} at \vf{90} rows, and falls to \vf{0.018} at \vf{900} rows.

The gain is small at the two ends of the curve for different reasons.
At \vf{9} rows, a single prompt gives either fit too little to learn from.
The hybrid fit then scores \vf{0.195} on NQ, a gain of \vf{0.036}, and on the two legal datasets it scores below the supervised fit, \vf{0.061} against \vf{0.081} on legal10w and \vf{0.021} against \vf{0.044} on legal100t.
At \vf{900} rows, the leak alone carries the supervised fit to \vf{0.743} on NQ and \vf{0.655} on legal10w, and little remains for matching to add.
