\section{Presence and Composition of the Leak}\label{sec:attack:ablations}\label{sec:attack:negative}

Two comparisons delimit the main result.
They vary the presence of the leak and its composition.

\subsection{Matching without a leak}\label{sec:attack:blind}

The matching term alone, without any leaked row, does not connect the target model.
At $\alpha = 0$, with the temperature and batch size of the main comparison, the map scores \vf{0.061} word-F1 on the selection prompts of NQ, against \vf{0.067} for its mismatched-prompt control.
The result covers one target model and one dataset.

\subsection{Composition of the leak}\label{sec:attack:leak}

On legal100t, a leak of \vf{90} rows yields a higher word-F1 when it is spread over more prompts, and the gain from matching stays near \vf{0.21} on the three leaks tested (Figure~\ref{fig:attack:shape}).
Three leaks take \vf{90} rows from \vf{2} prompts, \vf{9} rows from each of \vf{10} prompts, and \vf{1} row from each of \vf{90} prompts; only legal100t has prompts long enough for the first.
On the three leaks, the supervised fit scores \vf{0.049}, \vf{0.094}, and \vf{0.108}, and the hybrid fit scores \vf{0.255}, \vf{0.299}, and \vf{0.328}.

The gain shrinks when the rows carry little content.
A leak of \vf{90} stop-word rows gives the hybrid fit \vf{0.115} against \vf{0.056} for the supervised fit.
A leak of one repeated word leaves both fits near their mismatched-prompt controls, at \vf{0.061} and \vf{0.057}.
