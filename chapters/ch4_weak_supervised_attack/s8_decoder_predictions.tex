\section{The Training Objective and the Decoder's Predictions}\label{sec:attack:teacher}

Both terms of the objective in Equation~\ref{eq:attack:loss} compare mapped states with hub outputs.
Those mapped states then form the soft prompt of a frozen decoder, and the decoder reconstructs the prompt one token at a time.
The objective therefore rewards agreement with hub outputs, while reconstruction depends on what the decoder predicts from the soft prompt.
This section measures those predictions directly.

The measurement runs each decoder under teacher forcing.
The decoder receives the soft prompt and the true previous tokens, and it predicts the next token.
Cross-entropy scores that prediction against the true token, and a lower value means a better prediction.

Each decoder is evaluated under three conditions that differ only in the soft prompt.
In the reference condition, the soft prompt comes from the hub outputs of OLMo.
In the mapped condition, it comes from the mapped states of the target model, under the supervised fit or the hybrid fit.
In the language-model baseline, the decoder receives no soft prompt and predicts each token from the previous tokens alone.

The excess cross-entropy compares a condition with the reference condition: it is the cross-entropy of the condition minus the cross-entropy of the reference condition.
The excess is averaged over content words, the words outside a stop-word list.
The excess cross-entropy of the language-model baseline marks the level that a soft prompt must beat: a fit above that level predicts the true tokens worse than no soft prompt.

\paragraph{Matching lowers the excess cross-entropy.} At the \vf{90}-token leak, the hybrid fit has the lower excess cross-entropy on all three datasets (Figure~\ref{fig:attack:ce}).
The excess falls from \vf{7.73} nats per content word under the supervised fit to \vf{1.36} under the hybrid fit on NQ, from \vf{10.41} to \vf{3.35} on legal10w, and from \vf{5.58} to \vf{5.11} on legal100t.
The language-model baseline separates the two fits.
Its excess is \vf{6.08} nats on NQ, \vf{9.56} on legal10w, and \vf{5.29} on legal100t.
The supervised fit is above that level on every dataset, so its soft prompt predicts the true tokens worse than no soft prompt.
The hybrid fit is below that level on every dataset, by \vf{4.71} nats on NQ, \vf{6.21} on legal10w, and \vf{0.18} on legal100t.
The matching term therefore improves the decoder's next-token predictions on every dataset, and least on legal100t, where word-F1 is also lowest.
