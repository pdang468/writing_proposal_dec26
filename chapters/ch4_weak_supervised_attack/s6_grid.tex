\section{Supervision Fraction and Temperature}\label{sec:attack:grid}

The setting of the main comparison comes from a grid over the supervision fraction and the temperature at \vf{90} rows, and a second grid tests the setting at \vf{900} rows.

\subsection{The grid at 90 rows}\label{sec:attack:grid90}

The grid runs on the \vf{90}-row leak and the \vf{500} selection prompts of each dataset (Figure~\ref{fig:attack:grid}).
The selection rule takes the cell with the highest mean word-F1 over NQ and legal100t.
That cell is $\alpha = 0.9$, $\tau = 2d$, with a mean of \vf{0.534}.
The cell is the best on NQ, at \vf{0.768}.
On legal10w, which the selection rule leaves out, the same cell is again the best, at \vf{0.604}.
On legal100t the cell is close to the best, \vf{0.299} against \vf{0.314} at $\alpha = 0.8$, $\tau = 2d$.

All three datasets show the same dependence on temperature.
For $\alpha \le 0.95$, word-F1 peaks at $\tau = 2d$ and falls on either side, and several cells at a low temperature score below the supervised fit.
The matching term therefore helps only within a range of temperatures.
One setting serves all three datasets, although finding it required prompts with known text.

\subsection{The grid at 900 rows}\label{sec:attack:grid900}

The advantage of matching remains at \vf{900} rows, but it is smaller than at \vf{90} rows (Figure~\ref{fig:attack:grid-size}).
The grid is repeated on the selection prompts with the leak of \vf{9} rows from each of \vf{100} prompts.
The best cell gains \vf{0.089} over the supervised fit on NQ, \vf{0.104} on legal10w, and \vf{0.118} on legal100t, against \vf{0.401}, \vf{0.379}, and \vf{0.206} for the setting at \vf{90} rows.
The setting of the main comparison remains the best cell on NQ and scores about \vf{0.015} below it on legal10w, and on legal100t the best cell moves to $\alpha = 0.95$, $\tau = d$.
The best cell is chosen on the prompts that score it, so its gain is an upper estimate.
