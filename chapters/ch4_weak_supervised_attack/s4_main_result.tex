\section{Matching Improves Reconstruction from a Small Leak}\label{sec:attack:hybrid}\label{sec:attack:main}

Unpaired matching raises reconstruction word-F1 on all three datasets (Figure~\ref{fig:attack:standard}).
With a leak of \vf{90} rows, word-F1 on the \vf{4{,}500} evaluation prompts of NQ rises from \vf{0.354} under the supervised fit to \vf{0.766} under the hybrid fit, a gain of \vf{0.411}. % src: results/v7_2/main/w46-confirmation-s{1,2,3}.json; results/legal128w/v7_2/main/w46-confirmation-s{1,2,3}.json (means of three seeds); interval computed from results/{v7_2,legal128w/v7_2}/main/mixed-v7_9/confirmation/*/decoding.jsonl (per-prompt word-F1 averaged over three decoders and three seeds; paired bootstrap over 4,500 prompts, 2,000 resamples, seed 20261113) ; results/legal10w/v7_2/main/w46-confirmation-s{1,2,3}.json (supervised 0.1917, hybrid 0.5991; per seed 0.2229, 0.1989, 0.1532 and 0.6028, 0.5952, 0.5993); interval computed from results/legal10w/v7_2/main/mixed-v7_9/confirmation/*/decoding.jsonl (per-prompt word-F1 averaged over three decoders and three seeds; paired bootstrap over 4,500 prompts, 2,000 resamples, seed 20261113: +0.4074 [0.4033, 0.4118])
The gains on legal10w and legal100t are \vf{0.407} and \vf{0.219}, the smallest on the dataset of long prompts.
Both fits receive the same leak, so the gain comes from the two unpaired pools.

\paragraph{Control.} The hybrid scores depend on the prompt's own hidden states.
On the selection prompts, the hybrid fit scores \vf{0.768} against its mismatched-prompt control of \vf{0.108} on NQ, \vf{0.604} against \vf{0.074} on legal10w, and \vf{0.299} against \vf{0.073} on legal100t. % src: mixed-w46-std.json of each dataset, seeds.1.cells.*.final.macro_f1 and wrong_memory.macro_f1 (NQ 0.7679, 0.1081; legal10w 0.6040, 0.0739; legal128w 0.2993, 0.0725)
The supervised fit on legal100t, at \vf{0.094}, is close to its control of \vf{0.059}, so \vf{90} rows alone yield little beyond the control. % src: results/legal128w/v7_2/main/mixed-w46-std.json seeds.1.cells (0.0938, 0.0594)

\paragraph{Exact match and decoders.} On the evaluation prompts, exact match distinguishes the word-overlap gain from full-prompt recovery (Figure~\ref{fig:attack:decoders}).
Under the hybrid fit, exact match is \vf{0.21} on NQ and \vf{0.02} on legal10w, against zero under the supervised fit, and it rounds to zero on legal100t. % src: computed from results/{v7_2,legal10w/v7_2,legal128w/v7_2}/main/mixed-v7_9/confirmation/*/decoding.jsonl, field exact_match, three decoders and three seeds (hybrid means 0.2121, 0.0209, 0.0000; supervised 0.0001, 0, 0)
The three decoders also differ.
SmolLM scores the highest word-F1 on every dataset, and on legal100t the OLMo decoder falls to \vf{0.160}. % src: results/{v7_2,legal10w/v7_2,legal128w/v7_2}/main/w46-confirmation-s{1,2,3}.json cells.*.per_decoder, means of three seeds

\paragraph{Decoded examples.} The hybrid fit preserves content words that the supervised fit loses (Table~\ref{tab:attack:examples}).
Three NQ evaluation prompts, decoded through SmolLM, span high, intermediate, and low word-F1 of the hybrid fit.
The first prompt survives verbatim.
In the second, at a word-F1 of \vf{0.80}, one name degrades, from \emph{sweet daddy} to \emph{sweetheart}.
In the third, at \vf{0.40}, the opening \emph{who played} survives, while the name and the title of the show are lost.
Under the supervised fit, on the same leak, the opening question words survive and the content words of all three prompts are lost.
