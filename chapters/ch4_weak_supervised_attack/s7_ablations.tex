The comparisons of this section vary one aspect of the attack at a time: the size of the leak, the presence of the leak, the components of the loss, the composition of the leak, the target model, and a further training step.

\subsection{Size of the leak}\label{sec:attack:budget}

%% BIG CHANGE: polish; reword or relocate this passage for precision and flow.
Increasing the paired-prompt budget raises reconstruction word-F1 while reducing the gain from unpaired matching.
On Corpus A the supervised fit scores \vf{0.348} at a budget of \vf{10} and \vf{0.876} at \vf{1{,}000}, against a hub reference of \vf{0.946} (Table~\ref{tab:attack:hybrid}, Figure~\ref{fig:attack:budget}). % src: results/v7_2/main/mixed-v7_9-s5.json seed_summary.budgets.*.macro_f1 (0.3482, 0.8759); factorial.json controls.true_olmo_code (0.9459)
%% BIG CHANGE: leave the intermediate budget comparisons in the table.
The hybrid fit exceeds the supervised fit by \vf{0.309} word-F1 at a budget of \vf{10}, with the gain falling to \vf{0.002} at \vf{1{,}000}. % src: same file, seed_summary.budgets.*.cell_minus_benchmark
Unpaired matching thus yields its largest gain at the smallest tested budget; at \vf{300} the gain is small but still positive, with an interval of \vf{0.007} to \vf{0.013}.
The budgets stop at \vf{1{,}000} because no larger leak was run.

\subsection{Matching without a leak}\label{sec:attack:blind}

%% BIG CHANGE: polish; reword or relocate this passage for precision and flow.
Without leaked pairs, the tested blind maps yield word-F1 close to their mismatched-prompt controls.
Table~\ref{tab:attack:blind} lists four groups of blind maps, trained on the unpaired pools of Corpus A with Pythia as the target model.
The groups vary the score of the matching term, select the best of twelve further runs, replace the matching term by a Gromov-Wasserstein coupling that pairs the two pools by their internal distances, or set $\alpha = 0$ in Equation~\ref{eq:attack:loss}.
The largest gain of a blind map over its mismatched-prompt control is \vf{0.025} word-F1 (interval \vf{0.019} to \vf{0.030}), at a word-F1 of \vf{0.080} against a hub reference of \vf{0.946}. % src: results/v7_2/main/neighbourhood.json seeds.1.comparison.factorial (0.0801, 0.0555, +0.0247 [0.0187, 0.0304])
That map is the best of twelve runs, so its gain is biased upward.
The tested blind maps thus gain at most \vf{0.025} word-F1 over their controls, whereas the hybrid fit improves on the supervised fit.
The result covers two objectives, one target model, and one corpus, and does not show that no unpaired method can connect a target model.

\subsection{Components of the loss}\label{sec:attack:components}

The setting of the Corpus A budget curve was selected in five steps on the selection prompts with seed \vf{1}; seeds \vf{2} and \vf{3} then repeated every fit. % src: results/v7_2/main/mixed-v7_9-s{1..5}.json selection (selection_seed 1), setting.seeds
Each step changes one component, keeps the value with the highest mean word-F1 over budgets \vf{10}, \vf{30}, and \vf{100}, and passes it to the next step (Table~\ref{tab:attack:steps}).
Step \vf{5} tests a moment penalty, which penalizes the distance of the mean and the radius of the mapped states from those of the hub outputs.

\subsection{Training the map through the decoder}\label{sec:attack:decoderkl}
