Chapter~\ref{ch:hub} trained the translation hub with full access to the hidden states of its models.
An adversary has no such access to a target model: it observes the hidden states that the client transmits, and it holds neither the weights of the model nor a way to query it.
This chapter connects that target model to the frozen hub through an affine map.
The frozen projectors and decoders of the hub then reconstruct the target model's prompts from the output of the map.

The map learns first from a small leak: a few short pieces of text for which the adversary holds both the words and the target model's hidden states.
The leak alone is too small to fit the map well, because a few pieces of text cannot show how the hidden states of a whole corpus fit into the hub.
Yet hidden states without their text are plentiful, because the server receives the hidden states of every prompt that the client sends.
The adversary is also assumed to hold ordinary text of the same kind as those prompts, with no link to any of them.
Nothing says which hidden state goes with which text, but if the target model and the hub organize text in a similar way, the two collections should look alike once the map is right.
A matching term therefore trains the map so that the mapped hidden states, taken together, look like the hub outputs of that text.
The chapter measures what the matching term adds to the leak: with a small leak, it more than doubles mean word-F1 on all three datasets.
