\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:attack:experiments}

\paragraph{Target model.} The target model is Pythia-1B, observed at block \vf{12} (width \vf{2048}) with one vector per token. % src: configs/v7.yaml (unknowns.pythia: d3_block 12, width 2048)

\paragraph{Datasets and pools.} The experiments run on the three datasets of Chapter~\ref{ch:hub}, NQ, legal10w, and legal100t, each with its own frozen hub.
The \vf{40{,}000} training prompts of a dataset are divided once, at random, into two halves of \vf{20{,}000}.
One half supplies the target model's unpaired pool as hidden states, and the other half supplies the hub's unpaired pool as hub outputs of OLMo. 
A leak draws its prompts at random from all \vf{40{,}000} training prompts and skips a prompt of fewer than \vf{9} rows. % src: src/v7_2/anchors_linear.py (span_start_pairs: the first prompts in a random order that hold at least 9 rows) 

\paragraph{The 90-row leak.} In the main experiment, the adversary obtains a leak of \vf{90} rows, the first \vf{9} rows of each of \vf{10} prompts.
The leak exposes the datasets to different degrees.
On NQ, a prompt averages \vf{9} words, so \vf{9} rows expose almost the entire prompt.
On legal10w, a prompt of \vf{10} words holds about \vf{13} rows, so \vf{9} rows again expose most of it. % src: results/legal10w/v7_2/main/v8_6-summary.json rows[*] (1, 3, 5, 10, 30, 100 prompts: 12, 34, 55, 125, 390, 1305 rows; E +0.0055, +0.2723, +0.300, +0.332, +0.220, +0.051; job 1595870, seed 1)
On legal100t, a prompt is a window of about \vf{68} words, or \vf{86} rows, cut from a longer document, so the same \vf{9} rows expose only its opening words. % src: artifacts/legal128w/v3/manifests/main-splits.jsonl window_words, validation split (mean 67.6); results/legal128w/v7_2/main/v8_6-summary.json rows[*].pairs (8612 rows for 100 prompts)
The adversary's knowledge is therefore fixed at \vf{90} rows on every dataset, while the exposed fraction of each leaked prompt differs.

\paragraph{Score.} Evaluation follows Section~\ref{sec:hub:eval}.
Each of the three decoders generates greedily from the projected output of the map, up to the length of the soft prompt plus \vf{32} tokens.
The score is word-F1, averaged over prompts and over the three decoders, and word-F1 without a decoder's name denotes that three-decoder mean.
Exact match and decoded examples accompany word-F1 to separate word overlap from full recovery. 

\paragraph{Control and hub reference.} The mismatched-prompt control gives each decoder the mapped states of another prompt and scores the output against the original prompt.
The hub reference is the word-F1 of the three decoders on the hub outputs of OLMo, with no map involved, \vf{0.947} on NQ and \vf{0.982} on each legal dataset. % src: results/{v7_2,legal10w/v7_2,legal128w/v7_2}/main/hub-dev-eval-m32.json olmo_source_mean.hub (0.9468, 0.9821, 0.9821), 500 selection prompts, generation cap rows+32

\paragraph{Settings.} The hybrid fit has three settings, the supervision fraction $\alpha$, the temperature $\tau$, and the batch size $B$ of the matching term.
The \vf{5{,}000} validation prompts of a dataset are divided into two sets.
The selection prompts, \vf{500} in number, serve to choose the settings.
The evaluation prompts, the other \vf{4{,}500}, score the main comparison and take no part in that choice. % src: v7_run.py (subset_seed 20260903, dev_prompts 500); src/v7_2/context.py; results/v7_2/main/eval/confirmation/prompt_ids.json
The setting is $\alpha = 0.9$, $\tau = 2d$, and $B = 8{,}192$ on every dataset (Section~\ref{sec:attack:grid}).
