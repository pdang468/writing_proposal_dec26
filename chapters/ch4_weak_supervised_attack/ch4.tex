\chapter{Weakly Supervised Attack with a Small Leak}\label{ch:attack}

This chapter connects a language model to the frozen translation hub of Chapter~\ref{ch:hub}, and measures how much of the input prompt the frozen decoders then reconstruct from that model's hidden states.
An affine map sends the hidden states of that model, the target model, into the hub; the projectors and decoders of Chapter~\ref{ch:hub} reconstruct the prompt without any change.
The setting is weakly supervised: the attacker holds a small leak, a set of hidden states whose text is known, and a large pool of hidden states whose text is unknown.
The chapter makes two points.
First, a matching term that compares mapped hidden states with unpaired hub outputs improves a map fitted on the leak alone: with a leak of \vf{90} rows, mean word-F1 over three seeds on \vf{4{,}500} held-out Natural Questions prompts rises from \vf{0.35} to \vf{0.77}. % src: results/v7_2/main/w46-confirmation-s{1,2,3}.json (0.3544, 0.7659)
Second, in separate experiments, a language model that rewrites the decoders' reconstructions in a second round raises word-F1 above the mean of the three decoders.

Section~\ref{sec:attack:threat} states the threat model and Section~\ref{sec:attack:hypotheses} the two hypotheses.
Section~\ref{sec:attack:method} describes the map, the loss, and the second round, and Section~\ref{sec:attack:experiments} the experiments and the selection of the setting.
Sections~\ref{sec:attack:hybrid} and~\ref{sec:attack:rewriting} report the two main results, Section~\ref{sec:attack:ablations} the ablation study, and Section~\ref{sec:attack:discussion} the discussion and the limitations.
