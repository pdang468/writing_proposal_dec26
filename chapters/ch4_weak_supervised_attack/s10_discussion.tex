\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:attack:discussion}

This chapter asked whether an adversary who holds only a small leak can read the input of a model outside the hub through a lightweight affine map.
A leak of \vf{90} rows supervises the map, and unpaired matching refines it on data that carry no correspondence.

The results support the hypothesis that unpaired matching improves reconstruction from a small leak.
On held-out NQ prompts the supervised training on leaks alone reaches a word-F1 of \vf{0.354}, and with matching the same leak reaches \vf{0.766}, a gain of \vf{0.411}. % src: results/v7_2/main/w46-confirmation-s{1,2,3}.json
The same step raises word-F1 from \vf{0.192} to \vf{0.599} on legal10w, a gain of \vf{0.407}, and from \vf{0.102} to \vf{0.321} on legal100t, a gain of \vf{0.219}. % src: results/{legal10w,legal128w}/v7_2/main/w46-confirmation-s{1,2,3}.json
Word-F1 thus more than doubles on every dataset.

The attack trains only the affine map, while the hub and its decoders stay frozen.
The gain from matching therefore suggests that such a map can carry the structure of the target model's hidden states into the shared space of the hub.
The Platonic Representation Hypothesis predicts this similarity between models, here for a target model that took no part in hub training.

Three findings bound the gain.
First, the gain depends on the size of the leak, with a peak at \vf{45} to \vf{90} rows and a fall to \vf{0.089} at \vf{900} rows on NQ and on legal10w. % src: mixed-w46-std-L900.json of NQ and legal10w, seed 1 (0.0889, 0.0889)
Second, the gain requires a temperature within the range where the matching term can tell its candidates apart.
Third, matching does not replace the leak, since without one the tested blind maps exceed their mismatched-prompt controls by at most \vf{0.025} word-F1.

    A supporting experiment relates the objective to the decoder's predictions.
The objective measures agreement with hub outputs, whereas teacher-forced cross-entropy measures how well the decoder predicts the true tokens.
At the \vf{90}-row leak, matching lowers the excess cross-entropy on all three datasets, from \vf{7.73} to \vf{1.36} nats per content word on NQ and from \vf{5.58} to \vf{5.11} on legal100t. % src: teacher_forced-w46/summary.json of NQ and legal128w

The three decoders of the hub yield three reconstructions of each prompt.
In a second round, a language model receives the three reconstructions, and no hidden state, and rewrites them into one text.
On legal10w with a leak of \vf{90} rows, rewriting lifts word-F1 from \vf{0.627}, the mean of the three decoders, to \vf{0.745}. % src: Section sec:attack:rewriting
The gain costs the adversary no further access to the target model.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Two limitations bound the claims.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Settings chosen with known domain.} The supervision fraction and the temperature were selected by word-F1 on \vf{500} prompts of the known domain, and that score reads their text.
\item \textbf{Models and layer.} The results cover one block of one target model of about one billion parameters and three hub models, without a defense or a deployed system.
\end{itemize}

A second target model remains future work.
