\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:attack:discussion}

This chapter asked whether an adversary can reconstruct prompts from the hidden states of a model outside the hub.
The adversary is a server that receives those hidden states and cannot query the target model.
Its resources are the frozen hub, two unpaired pools from the domain under attack, the tokenizer of the target model, and a leak of \vf{90} rows.

The results support the hypothesis that unpaired matching improves reconstruction from a small leak.
On held-out prompts, the hybrid fit raises word-F1 over the supervised fit from \vf{0.354} to \vf{0.766} on NQ, from \vf{0.192} to \vf{0.599} on legal10w, and from \vf{0.102} to \vf{0.321} on legal100t. % src: results/{v7_2,legal10w/v7_2,legal128w/v7_2}/main/w46-confirmation-s{1,2,3}.json
The gains are \vf{0.411}, \vf{0.407}, and \vf{0.219}.
Full recovery remains challenging: exact match under the hybrid fit is \vf{0.21} on NQ and \vf{0.02} on legal10w, and it rounds to zero on legal100t. % src: Section sec:attack:main, exact match

The attack is lightweight, since it trains only one affine map.
The hub and its decoders stay frozen, so that map alone connects the target model to the hub.
The gain therefore suggests that an affine map can align the hidden states of the target model with the hub, even though the target model took no part in hub training.
Such an alignment is consistent with the Platonic Representation Hypothesis.

The other experiments of the chapter study that gain.
The gain peaks at \vf{45} to \vf{90} rows and falls to \vf{0.089} at \vf{900} rows on NQ and on legal10w, where the leak alone carries the supervised fit to \vf{0.743} and \vf{0.655}. % src: mixed-w46-std-L900.json of NQ and legal10w (gain 0.0889, 0.0889; supervised 0.7434, 0.6548)
Matching still needs the leak, since without one the tested blind maps on NQ exceed their mismatched-prompt controls by at most \vf{0.025} word-F1. % src: Section sec:attack:blind
The gain does not depend on how the \vf{90} rows are spread over prompts.
On legal100t, the \vf{90} rows were drawn from \vf{2}, \vf{10}, or \vf{90} prompts, and the gain stays near \vf{0.21} in all three cases.
The gain does depend on the temperature, and at \vf{90} rows word-F1 peaks at $\tau = 2d$. % src: Section sec:attack:grid90
The decoder's next-token predictions confirm the gain: under teacher forcing, the excess cross-entropy falls with matching on all three datasets, from \vf{7.73} to \vf{1.36} nats per content word on NQ. % src: teacher_forced-w46/summary.json of NQ

A second round extends the attack with no further access to the target model.
The three decoders of the hub yield three reconstructions of each prompt, and the reconstructions differ.
A language model receives the three reconstructions, and no hidden state, and rewrites them into one text.
Against the best single decoder, the rewrite gains \vf{0.117} word-F1 on legal100t and \vf{0.061} on legal10w, and it matches the decoder on NQ. % src: Section sec:attack:rewriting
The gain is thus largest where the decoders are weakest.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Two limitations bound the claims.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Settings chosen with known domain.} The supervision fraction and the temperature were selected by word-F1 on \vf{500} prompts of the known domain, and that score reads their text.
\item \textbf{Models and layer.} The results cover one block of one target model of about one billion parameters and three hub models, without a defense or a deployed system.
\end{itemize}

Within these limits, the chapter reduces to one result.
With about \vf{90} leaked words and one affine map, the hub's frozen decoders reconstruct held-out NQ prompts of a model outside the hub at a word-F1 of \vf{0.766}, against \vf{0.354} without matching.
A second target model remains future work.
