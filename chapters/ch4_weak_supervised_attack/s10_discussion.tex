\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:attack:discussion}

This chapter asked whether an adversary can reconstruct prompts from the hidden states of a model outside the hub.
The adversary is a server that receives those hidden states and cannot query the target model.
Its resources are the frozen hub, two unpaired pools from the domain under attack, the tokenizer of the target model, and a leak of \vf{90} rows.

With that leak, unpaired matching improves reconstruction.
On the evaluation prompts, the hybrid fit raises word-F1 over the supervised fit from \vf{0.354} to \vf{0.766} on NQ, from \vf{0.192} to \vf{0.599} on legal10w, and from \vf{0.102} to \vf{0.321} on legal100t.
The resulting gains are \vf{0.411}, \vf{0.407}, and \vf{0.219}.
The gain also peaks near this leak size, at \vf{45} to \vf{90} rows.
Despite those gains, full recovery remains challenging: exact match under the hybrid fit is \vf{0.21} on NQ and \vf{0.02} on legal10w, and it rounds to zero on legal100t.

The attack behind those gains is lightweight, since it trains only one affine map.
The gains thus suggest that one such map can align the hidden states of the target model with the hub, even though the target model took no part in hub training.

A second round extends the attack beyond the map, with no further access to the target model.
In that round, a language model rewrites the three reconstructions of the hub's decoders into one text, without any hidden state.
The rewrite raises word-F1 over the best single decoder on legal10w and legal100t, where the decoders are weakest, and adds \vf{0.008} on NQ.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Settings chosen with known domain.} The supervision fraction and the temperature were selected by word-F1 on \vf{500} prompts of the known domain.
\item \textbf{Models and layer.} The results cover one block of one target model and three hub models.
Other target models, other blocks, and defended systems need further study.
\end{itemize}
