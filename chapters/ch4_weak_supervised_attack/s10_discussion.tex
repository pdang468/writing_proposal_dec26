\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:attack:discussion}

This chapter asked whether an adversary can reconstruct prompts from the hidden states of a model outside the hub.
The adversary is a server that receives those hidden states and cannot query the target model.
Its resources are the frozen hub, two unpaired pools from the domain under attack, the tokenizer of the target model, and a leak of \vf{90} rows.

The results support the hypothesis that unpaired matching improves reconstruction from a small leak.
On held-out prompts, the hybrid fit raises word-F1 over the supervised fit from \vf{0.354} to \vf{0.766} on NQ, from \vf{0.192} to \vf{0.599} on legal10w, and from \vf{0.102} to \vf{0.321} on legal100t. % src: results/{v7_2,legal10w/v7_2,legal128w/v7_2}/main/w46-confirmation-s{1,2,3}.json
Word-F1 thus more than doubles on every dataset.
Full recovery is rare: exact match under the hybrid fit is \vf{0.21} on NQ and \vf{0.02} on legal10w, and it rounds to zero on legal100t. % src: Section sec:attack:main, exact match

The attack trains only the affine map, while the hub and its decoders stay frozen.
The frozen decoders recover words of the prompt from the mapped states, so staying outside hub training does not protect a model's prompts from partial recovery.
The result is consistent with the Platonic Representation Hypothesis.

Four findings bound the gain.
First, the gain peaks at \vf{45} to \vf{90} rows and falls to \vf{0.089} at \vf{900} rows on NQ and on legal10w. % src: mixed-w46-std-L900.json of NQ and legal10w (0.0889, 0.0889)
Second, at \vf{90} rows and $\alpha \le 0.95$, word-F1 peaks at $\tau = 2d$, and several cells at a lower temperature score below the supervised fit. % src: Section sec:attack:grid90
Third, on legal100t the gain stays near \vf{0.21} whether the \vf{90} rows come from \vf{2}, \vf{10}, or \vf{90} prompts. % src: Section sec:attack:leak
Fourth, without a leak, the tested blind maps on NQ exceed their mismatched-prompt controls by at most \vf{0.025} word-F1, so matching does not replace the leak. % src: Section sec:attack:blind

Matching also improves the decoder's next-token predictions under teacher forcing.
At the \vf{90}-row leak, the excess cross-entropy falls on all three datasets, from \vf{7.73} to \vf{1.36} nats per content word on NQ and from \vf{5.58} to \vf{5.11} on legal100t. % src: teacher_forced-w46/summary.json of NQ and legal128w
On every dataset, the supervised fit is above the unconditional baseline, the decoder with no soft prompt, and the hybrid fit is below it.

The three decoders of the hub yield three reconstructions of each prompt.
In a second round, a language model receives the three reconstructions, and no hidden state, and rewrites them into one text.
Against the best single decoder, rewriting gains \vf{0.117} word-F1 on legal100t and \vf{0.061} on legal10w, and it matches the decoder on NQ. % src: Section sec:attack:rewriting
The gain costs the adversary no further access to the target model.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Two limitations bound the claims.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Settings chosen with known domain.} The supervision fraction and the temperature were selected by word-F1 on \vf{500} prompts of the known domain, and that score reads their text.
\item \textbf{Models and layer.} The results cover one block of one target model of about one billion parameters and three hub models, without a defense or a deployed system.
\end{itemize}

A second target model remains future work.
